MK10 Sprocket and Chain Assembly

MK10 Sprocket and Chain Assembly is Bruin Formula Racing's front/rear sprockets, transmission shaft, and chain; as Sprocket Responsible Engineer, I designed and analyzed the full assembly.

Outcome

  • Delivered a sprocket and chain assembly -- a 59-tooth rear sprocket, 12-tooth front sprocket, and topology-optimized geometry -- that passed static analysis with a 2× factor of safety.
  • Switched to the smaller 428VX chain (from the prior year's 520), letting the sprockets sit closer together and increasing the final drive ratio -- simulations showed this would gain points on course.

Slides for Preliminary Design Review (PDR), Critical Design Review (CDR), and Final Design Review (FDR), showing the evolution of the front sprocket, rear sprocket, and motor shaft over the course of the past couple of months:

What I Did

  • Used SolidWorks to model the sprocket teeth geometry for a 59-tooth rear sprocket and a 12-tooth front sprocket, based on final drive ratio values simulated on OptimumLap.
  • Modeled the involute splines at the center of the sprockets and on the motor shaft to ensure proper alignment and functionality.
  • Ran a Topology Optimization on the rear sprocket using Altair Inspire, iterating over cutouts to reduce weight.
  • Ran static simulations on Ansys Mechanical to verify both sprockets passed with a 2x factor of safety, based on chain tension calculations.
  • Selected a 17 teeth 17 DP 45-degree pressure angle spline for the motor shaft (after evaluating a 30-degree pressure angle) and maximized the bore hole cutout, verified against failure using hand calculations from maximum shear and normal stress theories.
  • Selected the smaller 428VX chain over the previous year's 520 chain, allowing closer sprockets and a larger FDR that simulations indicated would maximize points.
