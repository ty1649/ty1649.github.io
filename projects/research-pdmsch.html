Xenopus Embryo Sorter Design

PDMS Chip/Housing is a microfluidics chip and hardware system I designed as the sole mechanical engineer on a Beel Research Group project automating the sorting of Xenopus eggs, which previously had to be checked and filtered by hand in batches of hundreds or thousands.

Outcome

The finished system is a transparent PDMS chip, cast from an SLA-printed mold, housed between two mirrors so a single camera captures both the top and bottom of the chip -- letting the machine learning model categorize each egg from one shot instead of two.

What I Did

I designed the hardware for the system for the ~1mm wide eggs that flow through the chip. Using a combination of sensors and pressure regulators, the eggs are stopped, photographed on both sides, and sorted with a machine learning model. As they flow out, sensors track each egg's path, and a quake valve sorter mechanism, selected after iterating through various sorting mechanisms, separates them.
